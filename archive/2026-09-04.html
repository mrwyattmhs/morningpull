Space & science

Recent Science Highlights

Roman Space Telescope Launch — NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched on Aug. 30 on a $4.3 billion mission atop a SpaceX Falcon Heavy. This is significant because it's an infrared space telescope for cosmology and search for exoplanets, positioned at Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2, making it a next-generation observatory comparable to Hubble.

Early Planet Formation Discovery — Rocky planets may have been able to start forming just 100 million years after the Big Bang, thanks to enormous early supernovae that scattered planet-building elements across space. This pushes back our timeline for planet genesis and suggests the universe was seeding planetary systems far earlier than previously thought.

Mars Heat Anomaly — Mars has a hidden temperature divide, with its southern interior potentially hundreds of degrees hotter than the north and partially molten. This discovery could reshape our understanding of Martian geology and internal structure.

Dark Matter Detection Begins — The Super Cryogenic Dark Matter Search (SuperCDMS) SNOLAB just began its scientific operations in Ontario, and over the next few years hopes to find direct evidence of "light" dark matter for the first time.

Quantum Heat Engine — A tiny superconducting engine has successfully converted heat near absolute zero into useful work, demonstrating the first cyclic quantum heat engine of its kind. This breakthrough could enable more efficient quantum computers.

Optical Chip Efficiency — Caltech scientists have created ultra-low-loss optical pathways on silicon chips that approach the efficiency of fiber optics and dramatically outperform existing technology at visible wavelengths.

Galactic Merger Ejects Black Hole — Researchers at UCSB and UT Austin have determined that a galactic merger kicked a supermassive black hole into intergalactic space, a rare and exotic outcome of cosmic collisions.

Pallas-1 Rocket Maiden Flight — Galactic Energy's new Pallas-1 rocket aced its debut launch on Monday (Aug. 31), successfully reaching orbit.
