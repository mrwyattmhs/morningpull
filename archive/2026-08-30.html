Recent Mathematics News

Major Breakthroughs

AI Solving Century-Old Problems

A team from Oxford and MIT used combinatorial design theory and quantum annealing to construct explicit Hadamard matrices for all orders up to 1000, then proved the conjecture for all orders. The Hadamard conjecture, which posits that a Hadamard matrix exists for every order divisible by 4, remained open for over a century. Why it matters: Hadamard matrices are fundamental in coding theory, quantum mechanics, and signal processing—solving this opens applications in error-correcting codes and experimental design.

An AI-generated proof disproves the Erdős unit distance conjecture, demonstrating that for infinitely many values of n, the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among n points in the plane can exceed certain bounds. It was the first historically significant proof to come from an AI model, though the model's result wasn't definitive—human mathematicians substantially improved on it within weeks. Why it matters: This 80-year-old geometry puzzle bears on how points distribute in space, relevant to combinatorial geometry and computer science.

Formal Verification of Classical Proofs

A collaboration between the Institute for Advanced Study and DeepMind used the AI system LeanMind to prove the Sylvester-Gallai conjecture—the first major open problem solved by AI without substantial human guidance. The proof was formally verified in Coq and published in the Annals of Mathematics. Why it matters: This marks a cultural shift: when machines generate proofs, formal verification becomes essential, raising the mathematical rigor bar.

Large AI Breakthrough Release

On August 1, 2026, OpenAI published a 249-page manuscript describing ten separate results in pure mathematics and theoretical computer science—each with a machine-checkable certificate written in the formal proof language Lean 4. OpenAI framed the effort as part of a push toward democratizing research access, offering free access to advanced models for 100,000 scientists and mathematicians worldwide.

Prizes & Recognition

The 2026 Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics was awarded to Frank Merle. Semyon Dyatlov, professor at MIT, received the 2026 AMS Bôcher Memorial Prize for results on the control of Laplace eigenfunctions on surfaces with Anosov geodesic flows, contributing to understanding quantum chaos. Monica Vişan received the inaugural 2026 Edmond and Nancy Tomastik Prize in Differential Equations for contributions to nonlinear dispersive and wave equations.

Open Problems

Major unsolved questions remain central to mathematics. The Riemann hypothesis, which concerns the distribution of prime numbers, is still considered one of the most important open problems in mathematics, promising to unlock hidden patterns. Six of the seven Millennium Prize Problems remain unsolved, while the Poincaré conjecture was proved by Perelman. The Erdős problems, with over 1,100 problems spanning combinatorics and number theory, represent a particularly rich testbed, as evidenced by recent AI contributions.
