COLLECTOR CAR MARKET DIGEST

Market Overview

The 2026 market shows a sharp divide: vehicles under $250,000 are losing value more often than gaining, while high-end vehicles have posted increases eight of the past 15 months. The Hagerty Market Rating sits at 59.01, remaining in "flat market" territory where it has hovered for nearly a year. Hagerty reported its collector car market rating sank to a 15-year low in early 2026, indicating the broad market lost heat from the post-COVID surge.

Notable Recent Auction Results

  • A 1996 Ferrari F50 set a model record at $14,575,000 at Mecum Monterey in August 2026
  • A 1960 Ferrari 250 GT SWB California Spider sold for $16.5 million at Gooding Christie's Amelia Island 2026
  • A 2003 Ferrari Enzo sold for $15 million and a 2005 Porsche Carrera GT for $6.7 million at Broad Arrow Amelia Island
  • A 2021 Ferrari Monza SP2 achieved a record $4,955,000, following appreciation from a $3,690,000 price in late 2025
  • Modern hypercars dominated top results across major auctions, though a 1961 Mercedes-Benz 300 SL Roadster led non-charity regular sales at Barrett-Jackson Scottsdale

Winners & Losers

After the pandemic boom, the market entered a selective phase with some segments softening, some flat, and others—especially modern enthusiast cars from the 1990s and 2000s—still rising.

Appreciating: - 1990s and early-2000s performance cars like Porsche 993-generation 911, Ferrari 360 Modena with manual gearbox, and BMW E46 M3 benefit from limited supply and rising nostalgia - 1980s and 1990s vehicles previously dismissed as "used cars" are transitioning to collectible status, including Porsche 964, early BMW M3s, and Japanese performance legends

Soft Spots: - Low-mileage premiums have tripled year-over-year while the middle market faces pressure

Modern Performance Cars Trending Toward Collectibility

Younger buyers want younger cars, with high-performance supercars of the '90s and later drawing more interest. The average model year of collector vehicles sold at auction in early 2026 is 1989, meaning the average age is now 37 years.

Hagerty's 2026 Bull Market List trends toward the '90s and '00s, reflecting modern enthusiast cars gaining popularity fast as vehicles representing the final chapter of the analog era with manual transmissions and limited electronic interference. The list includes the 1995–1998 Nissan Skyline GT-R at $82,350, 2006–2013 Corvette Z06 at $55,900, and 2004–2007 Porsche Carrera GT at $1.55 million.

Collectors are placing a premium on analog driving experiences, with manual transmissions, hydraulic steering, naturally aspirated engines, and simpler mechanical systems attracting enthusiasts as such features become rare in new vehicles.

Cadillac V-Series.R: Factory Motorsport Update

IMSA The V-Series.R enters its fourth year of competition, sporting an updated aero package and a familiar driver lineup. Cadillac earned victories in the final two races of the 2025 IMSA season. Three-car lineup: No. 10 Cadillac Wayne Taylor Racing with Ricky Taylor, Filipe Albuquerque (season), and Will Stevens (Daytona, Sebring, Road Atlanta); No. 40 Cadillac Wayne Taylor Racing with Jordan Taylor, Louis Deletraz (season), and Colton Herta (Daytona, Sebring, Road Atlanta); No. 31 Cadillac Whelen with Jack Aitken, Earl Bamber.

WEC & Le Mans Cadillac Hertz Team Jota competed in WEC for its first year in 2025. At a 2026 WEC race, Will Stevens took third place driving the No. 12 Cadillac Hertz Team JOTA V-Series.R, securing the first podium finish of 2026 for Cadillac Racing in the FIA World Endurance Championship.

For Le Mans (June 13-14), Cadillac Racing entered three Cadillac V-Series.R prototypes in the 2026 24 Hours of Le Mans. WEC entries No. 12 and No. 38 were shared by Jack Aitken, Earl Bamber, Sébastien Bourdais, Alex Lynn, Norman Nato, and Will Stevens, while No. 101 Cadillac V-Series.R was fielded by Wayne Taylor Racing with Ricky Taylor, Jordan Taylor, and Filipe Albuquerque. Cadillac Racing brought home a fourth-place finish at Le Mans, just missing the podium. The No. 38 car, with Bourdais leading at the midpoint, suffered a power steering issue and ultimately had to be retired.

Technical Developments Notable aero changes include removal of front dive planes and winglets and a reshaped rear wing profile intended to boost peak speed. Cadillac switched to full Brembo brakes after previously using only Brembo calipers.
