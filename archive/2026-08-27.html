Market Divisions

The ratio of value increases to decreases for vehicles under $250,000 has only increased once in 15 months, while high-end vehicles have increased eight months in the same period. The Hagerty Market Rating at 59.01 remains in "flat market" territory; the Median Sale Price dropped to its lowest value at 15.38.

Modern Cars Gaining Collectibility

The auction site Cars & Bids marked the beginning of amplified focus on newer enthusiast vehicles, driven partly by a shift in preferences toward reliability and usability in collector cars. Younger collectors are driving interest in 1980s–2000s models, trucks, and SUVs, while traditional American muscle cars saw value declines.

As modern vehicles become more digital and automated, collectors place a premium on analog driving experiences—manual transmissions, hydraulic steering, naturally aspirated engines, and simpler mechanical systems. Reshaped by electrification and regulations, modern performance cars are evolving rapidly, and certain models produced today could become sought-after collector cars in decades to come.

Specific Emerging Segments

Collectible trucks have gained momentum quickly, with GMT400 Chevrolet and GMC pickups built 1988–1998 becoming a major part of the collector landscape. Modern classics like the 2001 BMW M3 E46 and 2001 Ford Lightning are gaining traction as they become eligible for 25-year import exemptions in 2026.

Manual transmission performance cars

I'll search for recent news about manual transmission performance cars with a focus on America.## Manual Transmission Performance Cars: This Week's News

New Models & Returning Options

Porsche confirmed an MT Package for the 911 Carrera S — bringing back the six-speed manual after dropping it from that trim at the end of the 2025 model year. More significantly, McLaren revealed its McL 6GT supercar will be offered with a manual, the first McLaren production car to feature one since the F1 of the early 1990s.

The Honda Civic Si and Type R carry the performance banner for Honda in the 2026 model year, both with close-ratio 6-speed manual transmissions. The Mustang GT offers a 6-speed manual option, and the Dark Horse Mustang can be ordered with the TREMEC TR-3160 single overdrive 6-speed.

Last Chances—Models Being Discontinued

The Porsche 718 Cayman and Boxster end production in 2026, with replacement models expected to be electric-only or hybrid. The Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing, hand-built at Lansing Grand River, is a 668-horsepower supercharged V8 manual-transmission sedan standing alone as the final eight-cylinder manual sedan on the market—with the CT4 ending production in June 2026 and the CT5 following by late 2026.

Volkswagen confirmed that starting with the 2027 model year, the Jetta GLI will exclusively feature the 7-speed DSG dual-clutch transmission, making the 2026 Jetta GLI the absolute final new Volkswagen you can buy with three pedals in the United States.

The Bigger Picture

Twenty-four models remain in 2026, with some scheduled to disappear by 2027. But here's the nuance: within that tiny sliver of the market, enthusiasts are choosing manuals at shocking rates, yet manuals are disappearing not because enthusiasts abandoned them, but because manufacturers decided the economics don't justify offering them across entire lineups.

Porsche sells 83% of its GT3 Touring models with manuals, 90% of BRZ buyers choose three pedals, and over half of Blackwing buyers opt for the stick shift.

The Future

As automakers pour resources into electrification, even performance icons are trading clutch pedals for instant electric torque. Toyota plans to market a stick shift for electric cars as soon as 2026, with prototypes simulating shifts complete with shifting sounds and capability to stall under certain circumstances.

The future of manual cars is uncertain, yet they are unlikely to disappear entirely; while their presence in the mainstream market continues to diminish, manual transmissions will likely persist in niche segments.
