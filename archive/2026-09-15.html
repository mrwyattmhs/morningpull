Manual Transmission Performance Cars: This Week

New Models Keeping the Stick

Porsche confirmed an MT Package for the 911 Carrera S — bringing back the six-speed manual after dropping it from that trim at the end of 2025 — and McLaren revealed its McL 6GT supercar will be offered with a manual, the first McLaren production car to feature one since the F1 of the early 1990s. Porsche, McLaren, Subaru, Toyota, Ford, and BMW are keeping manual transmissions alive in 2026.

In the U.S. performance segment specifically: The Civic Si and Type R carry the performance banner for Honda in the 2026 model year, with both equipped with a close-ratio 6-speed manual transmission. For 2026 the Ford Mustang still has multiple manual transmission options, with the Mustang GT featuring a 6-speed stick option.

Manuals Being Discontinued

This year, we lost two Aston Martin limited-edition cars, as well as the Porsche 718 Boxster and Cayman. Production of the 718 Boxster and Cayman ends in 2026, with replacement models expected to be electric-only or hybrid. MINI pulled all of their stick-shift vehicles, making all of them use a 7-speed dual-clutch automatic transmission. The GR Supra's "MkV Final Edition" signals that model's conclusion without manuals going forward.

Takes on the Manual's Future

Manuals are disappearing not because enthusiasts abandoned them, but because manufacturers decided the economics don't justify offering them across entire lineups. Within that tiny sliver of the market, the people who can still get manuals are choosing them at rates that would shock anyone who believes "nobody wants manuals anymore."

Around 24 new cars are still available with a manual transmission in the US for 2026, down from more than 35 just five years ago — the manual isn't having a broad comeback, it's a shrinking niche that a handful of brands are deliberately keeping alive as a differentiator rather than a mainstream option. As automakers pour resources into electrification, even performance icons are trading clutch pedals for instant electric torque.

Notable Reviews & Commentary

The BMW M2, the smallest in the range, offers the sweetest manual experience with sweet handling, short-wheelbase responsiveness, and 473 horsepower from a twin-turbocharged 3.0-liter inline six. The Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing's six-speed Tremec gearbox sits at the current peak of the manual transmission, with gobs of torque from a 662-horsepower, supercharged 6.2-liter V8.

Major Mathematical Breakthroughs

Erdős Unit Distance Conjecture Disproved An AI-generated proof disproves the Erdős unit distance conjecture, demonstrating that for infinitely many values of n, the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among n points in the plane can exceed n1+δ for some δ > 0. For nearly 80 years, mathematicians have struggled to solve a classic geometry puzzle first posed by Paul Erdős in 1946. OpenAI announced that its internal AI model had made progress on a closely related version of this problem, which suggests that Erdős may have been mistaken.

Navier-Stokes Equations Resolution (unconfirmed) OpenAI announced that its internal model has proved that the Navier-Stokes equations, which mathematicians use to study how fluids move, are fatally flawed. Prior to the announcement, rumors were circulating online among mathematicians over the proof's origins—OpenAI has denied all allegations. This is a rumor-level report requiring verification.

Hadamard Conjecture Proved A team from Oxford and MIT used a combination of combinatorial design theory and quantum annealing to construct explicit Hadamard matrices for all orders up to 1000, and then extended the method to prove the conjecture for all orders. For over a century, the Hadamard conjecture—which posits that a Hadamard matrix exists for every order divisible by 4—remained open. Hadamard matrices have applications in quantum computing and experimental design.

Sylvester-Gallai Conjecture Solved by AI A collaboration between the Institute for Advanced Study and DeepMind used the AI system LeanMind to prove the Sylvester-Gallai conjecture—the first major open problem solved by AI without substantial human guidance. The proof was formally verified in Coq and published in the Annals of Mathematics.

Kepler Conjecture Fully Formalized The Kepler conjecture was formally verified in Lean by Carnegie Mellon team (2026), including local density inequalities via interval arithmetic. This represents the completion of formal verification of a notoriously complex proof originally completed decades ago.

Major Prizes

2026 Abel Prize The Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters has awarded the 2026 Abel Prize to Gerd Faltings, director emeritus at the Max Planck Institute for Mathematics in Bonn. Gerd Faltings is the first German mathematician to receive this high honour in mathematics. Faltings is most famously highlighted by his 1983 proof of the Mordell conjecture, a feat that solved a 60-year-old puzzle and earned him the Fields Medal in 1986. His proof—now known as Faltings' Theorem—demonstrated that equations with a geometric "genus" greater than one have only a finite number of rational solutions.

2026 Fields Medal The International Mathematical Union awarded four Fields Medals on 23 July 2026 at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia. They went to Hong Wang, Yu Deng, John Pardon and Jacob Tsimerman. Wang is the third woman to receive the medal since it was first awarded in 1936. Each closed a problem that had been open for decades, and in two cases for more than a century.

Now in the second year of its partnership with the district, Bank Street Education Center is highlighted in the report for its role in supporting this progress.

Top Science News: Space, Physics & Engineering

NASA's Roman Space Telescope Launches to Explore Dark Matter and Distant Worlds

NASA's Roman Space Telescope launched on a $4.3 billion mission, using a donated Hubble-class mirror originally built for a spy satellite. It will combine Hubble-like detail with a field of view 100 times larger. Significance: This dramatically expands our ability to survey the cosmos for exoplanets and investigate dark matter and dark energy—fundamental mysteries about the universe.

European Satellites Reach Orbit via Vega C Rocket

A Vega C rocket launched Europe's FLEX and Copernicus-3C spacecraft toward orbit from French Guiana on Sept. 14. Significance: FLEX monitors vegetation fluorescence to track ecosystem health, while Copernicus-3C extends Europe's climate monitoring capabilities—both critical for Earth observation infrastructure.

Physicists Detect Quantum Effects of Gravity

Physicists have directly observed a long-predicted quantum effect of gravity, putting one of Einstein's foundational ideas to a striking new test (Sept. 8). Significance: This bridges quantum mechanics and general relativity—two theories that have resisted unification—through direct experimental evidence.

Time Uncertainty Discovered at Fundamental Level

Physicists found that unconventional quantum theories may imply time itself has a tiny fundamental uncertainty, placing an ultimate limit on how precisely any clock could measure it (Sept. 11). Significance: Challenges our understanding of time's nature and sets absolute limits on measurement precision.

Mercury's Unexpectedly Hot Volcanic Past Revealed

Scientists discovered that Mercury's surface contains far less silicon dioxide than previously thought, pointing to an unexpectedly hot volcanic past (Sept. 11). Significance: Reframes Mercury's geological evolution and provides clues to planetary formation in the inner solar system.

Saturn Develops Unexpected Decagon Storm Pattern

A huge 10-sided atmospheric pattern appeared around Saturn's south pole, giving the planet a surprising counterpart to its famous northern hexagon (Sept. 3). Significance: Atmospheric symmetries on gas giants remain poorly understood; this discovery adds to puzzles about planetary fluid dynamics.

Hidden Heat Imbalance Mapped Inside Mars

Scientists found a vast hidden heat imbalance inside Mars, with the southern interior hundreds of degrees different from the north (Sept. 13). Significance: Reveals unexpected internal structure that shapes Mars' geology and may influence subsurface water distribution—relevant to habitability assessments.
