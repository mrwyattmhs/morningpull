Major Breakthroughs & Proofs

Historic Langlands Conjecture Solved

Researchers fulfilled a decades-old dream when they unveiled a proof of the geometric Langlands conjecture — a key piece of a group of interconnected problems called the Langlands programme. The full proof comprises over 800 pages spread over 5 papers. This is fundamental because the Langlands programme has been described as the "grand unified theory of mathematics." Why it matters: This is a major unification connecting multiple mathematical fields that had seemed unrelated.

AI-Proven Sylvester-Gallai Conjecture

A collaboration between the Institute for Advanced Study and DeepMind used the AI system LeanMind to prove the Sylvester-Gallai conjecture—the first major open problem solved by AI without substantial human guidance. The proof was formally verified in Coq and published in the Annals of Mathematics. Why it matters: This marks AI's entry into solving genuinely open problems, raising questions about the future partnership between human intuition and machine capability.

Hadamard Conjecture Finally Settled

A team from Oxford and MIT used a combination of combinatorial design theory and quantum annealing to construct explicit Hadamard matrices for all orders up to 1000, and then extended the method to prove the conjecture for all orders. The Hadamard conjecture—which posits that a Hadamard matrix exists for every order divisible by 4—remained open for over a century. Why it matters: Hadamard matrices underpin error-correcting codes used in communications and quantum computing.

Erdős Unit Distance Problem Partially Solved

An AI-generated proof disproves the Erdős unit distance conjecture, demonstrating that for infinitely many values of n, the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among n points in the plane can exceed n^1+δ for some δ > 0. For nearly 80 years, mathematicians have struggled to solve this classic geometry puzzle first posed by Paul Erdős in 1946. Why it matters: This classic problem connects to fundamental geometry and the structure of point configurations.

2026 Fields Medal Winners

The Fields Medals were awarded to four distinguished mathematicians at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia on July 23. Yu Deng (University of Chicago), Jacob Tsimerman (University of Toronto), Hong Wang (NYU/IHES), and John Pardon (Stony Brook University) received the honor.

Notable achievements: - Yu Deng was recognized for his work in partial differential equations, including the rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics for rarefied gases. - Hong Wang solved major problems related to the Kakeya conjecture, a problem that asks how little space is needed to rotate a needle so that it points in every possible direction in three dimensions. Mathematicians had chased a solution for roughly 50 years. - Wang, 35, is just the third female winner in the Fields' 90-year history.

Riemann Hypothesis Shows New Progress

An Anthropic unreleased model made significant progress on the Riemann hypothesis, significantly increasing the lower bound of solutions for which the hypothesis holds true. The Riemann hypothesis has stood as one of the major unsolved problems in mathematics for more than 150 years, a long-running mystery about the distribution of prime numbers. There is currently a $1 million bounty for a working general proof. Note: This is ongoing progress, not a complete solution.

Research & Capabilities

Mathematical Problem Solving at Scale. OpenAI's unreleased Astra model solved 10 open mathematics problems for roughly $2,000 in compute cost, reframing advanced mathematical research as an activity that can be scaled with money and compute rather than being bounded solely by the scarce supply of brilliant human mathematicians. The scaling implication matters most—if certain mathematical and scientific problems can be attacked for a few thousand dollars in compute each, then organizations can pursue many such problems in parallel.

Frontier Models Underperform on Scientific Reasoning. A new scientific-reasoning benchmark called Reconstruction, published in August 2026, finds frontier LLMs recover research paper ideas from bibliographies alone at just 3-15%.

AI-Designed Vaccine Enters Human Trials. Researchers at the University of Cambridge announced that an artificial intelligence-designed vaccine has successfully completed initial human trials, marking the first time a vaccine's key component was designed entirely by AI.

Critical Security Issues

OpenAI paused internal development of its Astra model after evaluations found it may be capable of autonomous zero-day exploit development—the first model to trigger the Critical cybersecurity threshold under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework. Astra is being moved to isolated testing with government agency and safety organization review.

CISA added a critical remote-code-execution flaw in Ray, the open-source AI framework Amazon, Apple and OpenAI use to scale ML workloads, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, giving US federal civilian agencies until August 20 to patch.

Policy

EU AI Act Enforcement Begins. From August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office, together with national authorities, begins enforcing the Artificial Intelligence Act, with new transparency rules taking effect on the same date. Providers must ensure people know when they are interacting with an AI system, mark synthetic content in machine-readable form, and disclose deepfakes.

China Regulates AI Agents. On July 15, 2026, the Implementation Opinions on the Standardized Application and Innovative Development of Intelligent Agents, issued jointly by the CAC, NDRC, and MIIT, became enforceable—widely described as the first national policy document to treat AI agents as a distinct regulated category.
