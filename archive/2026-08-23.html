The State of Play

Only around 24 new car models offer manual transmissions in 2026, a steep decline from earlier in the decade. Those who can still get manuals are choosing them at high rates, but manuals are disappearing not because enthusiasts abandoned them, but because manufacturers decided the economics don't justify offering them across entire lineups.

New Models Keeping the Stick

American Performance: - The 2025 Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing features a supercharged 6.2-liter V8 pumping out 668 horsepower with a six-speed manual - The Mustang GT with 5.0L V8 has a 6-speed stick option, and the Dark Horse Mustang can be ordered with the TREMEC TR-3160 single overdrive 6-speed

Japanese & International: - Honda continues offering several enthusiast-geared manual transmission options, such as the 2026 Honda Civic Si and 2025 Acura Integra Type S - The Acura Type S is only available with a six-speed manual transmission - The 2026 M2 has rear-wheel drive and a standard six-speed manual gearbox - The Porsche 911 Carrera T is manual-only with a 388-horsepower flat-six; a higher-performance 911 GT3 uses a 502-horsepower flat-six and can be had with a six-speed manual

Trucks: The Toyota Tacoma is the only pickup still offered new with a manual gearbox, making it the lone survivor in a market that once treated stick shifts as standard equipment.

Discontinuations & Final Years

Manual transmissions are being discontinued after the 2026 model year for the Volkswagen Jetta GLI, BMW M3/M4, BMW Z4, Toyota GR Supra, Lotus Emira, Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing, and Cadillac CT4-V Blackwing. The Porsche 718 Cayman and Boxster end production in 2026, with replacement models expected to be electric-only or hybrid.

The Future Outlook

The automotive industry's rapid shift toward hybrid powertrains fundamentally conflicts with manual transmissions; electric motors provide instant torque that automatic transmissions manage more effectively, making manuals technically impractical as hybridization spreads.

The Porsche 911 remains one of the most prominent defenders of the manual; Porsche has publicly stated that it intends to keep the manual available as long as customer demand justifies it.
