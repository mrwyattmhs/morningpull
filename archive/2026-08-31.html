I'll search for recent information on high-performance driver education and track driving.## TRACK DRIVING & HPDE

Driver Education Overview

HPDE (High Performance Driver Education) is one of the best ways for enthusiasts to safely explore their vehicle's capabilities, improve their driving skills, and have fun on a racetrack. Drivers receive instruction, follow passing rules, and participate in controlled environments designed to help them become faster, smoother, and safer behind the wheel. Most HPDE events are organized into skill groups ranging from beginners to advanced drivers. New participants typically attend classroom sessions and receive in-car instruction from experienced coaches. Throughout the day, drivers rotate between track sessions and educational briefings, learning concepts such as braking technique, cornering, vehicle dynamics, and racing line fundamentals.

TECHNIQUE: BRAKING

Trail braking blends braking and turning at the same time, enabling a driver to brake slightly later while manipulating the car's mass to aid corner entry. Many novice drivers do not use the full braking capability of their cars. For advanced drivers, left foot braking and trailbraking permit much quicker corner entries and greatly improved control of the car.

Threshold braking (or limit braking) is a technique most commonly used in motor racing to slow a vehicle at the maximum rate using the brakes. The technique involves the driver controlling the brake pedal pressure to maximize the braking force developed by the tires, applied at the point when the wheel just begins to slip.

TIRES & BRAKES FOR TRACK USE

Tires: For a first few track days, high-performance summer tires or entry-level R-compounds give predictable behavior. Street-legal semi-slicks are ideal if you occasionally drive home.

Brakes: Brakes turn speed into heat. On the track, preventing fade and keeping consistent pedal feel are essential. Replace old, glazed pads with a track-oriented compound. Use slotted or drilled rotors if you expect heavy, repeated heat cycles, and consider larger or vented rotors if you're running a heavy car at high speeds. Brake fluid with a higher boiling point reduces the chance of vapor lock under repeated heavy braking.

Inspection: Monitor uneven wear across the whole tread, scuffing or wear past the edge of the tread, and torn tread visible as a faint line across the tread.

DATA & COACHING TOOLS

RaceCapture shows predictive lap times with real-time +/- delta feedback vs. your best lap and instantly review your laps right as you come off track through the RaceCapture app. Live-stream racing to Podium for real-time analysis on engine, chassis, and driver performance, and share and compare laps with race coaches worldwide.

Motion Metric lets you use the same platform and analysis tools for both sim racing and track days, compare real track laps with sim data side-by-side, and upload in-car footage synced with telemetry for comprehensive coaching sessions.

Trophi.ai provides real-time, voice-guided tips through your headset like having a professional race engineer in your ear, correcting shift points and braking zones while you are still on the track. onTrackTools brings professional-grade motorsport telemetry analysis to your Mac, turning raw track-day data into clear, actionable insight for refining driving skills or validating car setup changes.

Manual transmission performance cars

MANUAL TRANSMISSIONS: THE WEEK'S NEWS

CARS KEEPING THE STICK (FOR NOW)

About 25 cars remain available with manual transmissions in the U.S., ranging from off-road SUVs to sports cars, sedans, and a pickup truck. The Toyota Tacoma is the only pickup still offered new with a manual gearbox.

When asked how many shoppers opted for manuals in 2026, some automakers reported increases compared to 2025—including BMW, Cadillac, Mazda, and Porsche. BMW saw strong take rates: 40% of M2 buyers and 50% of Z4 buyers chose manuals, while 50% of rear-wheel-drive M3 buyers and 33% of M4 buyers selected the stick shift.

MAJOR DISCONTINUATIONS

Manuals being discontinued after 2026 include the Volkswagen Jetta GLI, BMW M3/M4, BMW Z4, Toyota GR Supra, Lotus Emira, Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing, and Cadillac CT4-V Blackwing. The Cadillac CT4 ends production in June 2026, with the CT5 following by late 2026, killing the 668-horsepower supercharged V8 Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing—the final eight-cylinder, manual-transmission sedan on the market.

Volkswagen confirmed the 2027 Jetta GLI will exclusively feature a dual-clutch automatic, making the 2026 model the absolute final new Volkswagen with three pedals in the U.S.

Before the M3 exits, BMW offers the M3 CS Handschalter (German for "hand shifter") this autumn with just 40 units for Canada, packing a six-speed manual and rear-wheel drive.

FUTURE OUTLOOK

Manuals are disappearing not because enthusiasts abandoned them, but because manufacturers decided the economics don't justify offering them across entire lineups. The industry's rapid shift toward hybrid powertrains fundamentally conflicts with manual transmissions; as hybridization spreads, manuals become technically impractical rather than simply unprofitable.

On a brighter note: A manual Si will most likely be available for the 2028 model year, according to Honda comments from August. Porsche North America CEO stated the company will offer manuals as long as legally allowed and customer demand exists, with demand expected to continue well into the next decade.
