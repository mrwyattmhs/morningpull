Breakthrough Proofs & Results

AI-Assisted Proofs Reaching Maturity

In February 2026, a competition called First Proof gave AI models a week to solve 10 research-level math problems unlikely to have appeared in their training data, with the models succeeding in solving over half the problems. OpenAI's ChatGPT-5.2 (Thinking) independently solved a mathematical problem involving a 2024 conjecture by mathematicians Ran and Teng. Why it matters: AI is transitioning from calculation tool to research collaborator, with human mathematicians now asking it to fill in proof details rather than verify simple arithmetic.

Twin Primes Conjecture—Conditional Breakthrough

A Princeton team improved the bounded prime gap result to a distance of 2—the full twin prime conjecture—but under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. The proof introduces a new sieve method based on additive combinatorics and non-trivial bounds on exponential sums. Why it matters: While conditional, this is a major step forward on a centuries-old problem. The twin prime conjecture asks whether infinitely many pairs of primes differ by exactly 2 (like 11 and 13).

Disproving a 150-Year Geometry Rule

Mathematicians found two different doughnut-shaped surfaces that look identical when measured locally but are actually different overall. Why it matters: This overturns a long-standing assumption in differential geometry about when local properties determine global structure.

Electoral Systems Theorem

Mathematicians showed that no electoral system can perfectly balance local representation, proportional national results, and a fixed-size parliament once enough parties compete. Why it matters: Formalizes an inherent trade-off in democratic design.

Major Prizes & Honorees

2026 Fields Medals (Math's Nobel Prize)

The 2026 Fields Medals went to Yu Deng (University of Chicago), John Pardon (Stony Brook University), Jacob Tsimerman (University of Toronto), and Hong Wang (NYU/IHES).

  • Hong Wang is the third woman ever to win the Fields Medal in the 90 years since the award was established. She solved the Kakeya conjecture in three dimensions—a problem mathematicians had chased for roughly 50 years asking how little space is needed to rotate a needle so it points in every direction.
  • Deng and Wang are the first Chinese-born winners of the Fields Medal since 1982.
  • Deng's work includes rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics and wave kinetic equations from nonlinear dispersive systems.

Other Major Awards

  • Hong Wang received the 2026 New Horizons in Mathematics Prize for work in harmonic analysis, partial differential equations, geometric measure theory, including the local smoothing conjecture and Furstenberg set conjecture.
  • Shayan Oveis Gharan (University of Washington) received the Abacus Medal for mathematical contributions to computer science.
  • Graeme Segal (University of Oxford) won the Chern Medal for outstanding lifetime achievement.
  • Yurii Nesterov (University of Louvain, Belgium) received the Gauss Prize for mathematical contributions with significant applied applications.
  • The 2026 Abel Prize went to Gerd Faltings (Max Planck Institute for Mathematics in Bonn).
